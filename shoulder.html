A comfortable range of motion

Gentle shoulder movement can support everyday comfort.

Your shoulders help with reaching, lifting, dressing, and many everyday tasks. Comfortable movement and gradual strengthening can help you stay confident using them.

Everyone's range and needs are different. Start with movements that feel manageable, keep them controlled, and avoid forcing a stretch or lifting through pain.

Choose a gentle starting point

Try these simple shoulder movements

01

Shoulder rolls

Sit or stand comfortably and slowly roll your shoulders forward, up, and back. Repeat a few times, then change direction without straining.

02

Wall-assisted reach

Face a wall and gently walk your fingers upward only as far as feels comfortable. Pause briefly, then walk them back down.

03

Light strengthening

Keep your elbows close to your sides and gently draw your shoulder blades back. Hold for a few seconds, relax, and repeat at an easy pace.

Make movement part of your day

Small, regular movement can fit into everyday routines.

Take a comfortable movement break between tasks, practise a few shoulder rolls while seated, or follow a gentle routine with someone you trust. Keep the movements easy and adjust them to suit how you feel.

Take a movement breakChange position and gently move your shoulders after sitting for a while.
Adjust your setupKeep frequently used items within a comfortable reach when possible.
Build graduallyIncrease repetitions or range little by little, only when the movement stays comfortable.

Listen to your body

Keep shoulder movement gentle and safe.

  • Move slowly and stay within a comfortable range; do not force a stretch.
  • Begin with a few repetitions and take breaks as needed.
  • Stop if you feel sharp or increasing pain, numbness, weakness, or unwell.
  • For ongoing symptoms, an injury, or individual exercise guidance, speak with a qualified health professional.

Your next step

Start with a few easy repetitions.

Choose one exercise, try a small number of controlled repetitions, then pause and notice how your arms feel. Keep it comfortable and build up only when you are ready.
