Understand visual comfort

Long periods of close-up work can tire your eyes.

Reading, studying, and using screens for long periods can leave your eyes feeling tired or dry. Glare, small text, and fewer blinks during concentrated work may add to discomfort.

Regular breaks and a comfortable setup can help reduce strain. These habits do not correct eyesight or replace an eye examination; ongoing symptoms deserve advice from an optometrist or other qualified health professional.

Pause and refresh

Try these simple eye breaks

01

Look into the distance

Try the 20-20-20 reminder: about every 20 minutes, look at something roughly 20 feet (6 metres) away for 20 seconds. Choose a comfortable distant object, never the sun.

02

Blink gently

Pause your task and blink naturally a few times, letting your eyelids close fully without squeezing. Blinking helps spread tears across the surface of your eyes.

03

Rest away from screens

Put your device down for a short break. Look around the room or gently close your eyes for a moment without pressing on them, then return when you feel ready.

Make your setup comfortable

Small screen changes can support your eyes.

Adjust your workspace so you can read without leaning forward or squinting. Combine short eye breaks with a change of posture or a walk away from your desk.

Reduce glareMove your screen away from reflections and adjust lighting and brightness for comfortable viewing.
Make text readableIncrease text size and keep your screen at a comfortable distance, around an arm's length for a desktop monitor.
Remember your breaksUse a gentle reminder to pause close-up work and look into the distance regularly.

Notice changes in your vision

Keep eye care gentle and seek help when needed.

  • Do not press or rub your eyes, force eye movements, or look directly at the sun.
  • Stop any practice that causes pain, dizziness, or blurred vision.
  • Arrange an eye examination for persistent discomfort, headaches, or difficulty seeing clearly.
  • Seek urgent medical care for sudden vision loss, severe eye pain, or new flashes or floaters, especially with a shadow or curtain across your vision.

Your next step

Take one short eye break today.

Pause your screen task, look into the distance, and blink gently. Before returning, check that your text size and lighting feel comfortable.
