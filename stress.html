The basics

Stress is a signal, not a personal failure.

Stress is your body’s natural response to pressure, uncertainty, or change. In short bursts, it can help you focus and respond quickly. When pressure continues without enough recovery, your mind and body can stay switched on for too long.

Understanding stress is not about removing every difficult feeling. It is about noticing what is happening early and choosing a response that supports your needs.

Notice the pattern

How it shows up

01

In the body

  • Tight jaw, shoulders, or chest
  • Headaches or stomach upset
  • Trouble falling or staying asleep
  • Fatigue that rest doesn't fix
  • Getting sick more often than usual
02

In mind and behavior

  • Irritability or a short fuse
  • Racing or looping thoughts
  • Trouble concentrating
  • Withdrawing from people
  • Changes in appetite

One sign does not tell the whole story. Look for changes from your usual pattern and be curious rather than critical.

Three gentle resets

Make the next minute easier.

01

Slow your breathing

Relax your shoulders and breathe in for four counts. Breathe out for six. Repeat for five rounds.

02

Notice your surroundings

Name five things you can see, four you can feel, and three you can hear. Let your attention return to now.

03

Choose one next step

Drink some water, step outside, or message someone you trust. One manageable action is enough for now.

Longer-term care

Create more room to recover.

01

Protect the basics

Regular meals, water, movement, and a consistent sleep routine give your nervous system a steadier foundation.

02

Make the load visible

Write down what is taking your energy. Separate what needs action today from what can wait or be shared.

03

Stay connected

Tell someone you trust how you are doing. Support can be practical, emotional, or simply a reminder that you do not have to manage everything alone.

When to reach out

If stress is affecting your daily life, sleep, relationships, or ability to cope, consider speaking with a health professional or counsellor. If you feel unsafe or might hurt yourself, contact local emergency services or a crisis support service immediately.
